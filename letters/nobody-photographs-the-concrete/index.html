Nobody Photographs the Concrete

Sentiment is at extreme fear. The foundations are still being poured. A letter on crypto, AI, and what lasts after the noise.

I first came across Bitcoin in 2014. What caught me wasn't the price. It was the idea: a ledger nobody owns, that anyone can check, and that moves value across the world without asking permission.

At my previous startup, we built software that carried orders and payments for restaurants and hotels. Every day I watched money travel through layers: banks, gateways, card networks, fees, settlement delays. Each layer took a little time and a little money. Each had a door someone could close.

Bitcoin was the first time I saw money as software.

The floor, not the house

When people talk about crypto, they talk about the house: the price, the new coins, the people who got rich or got wiped out. That's the part that gets photographed.

I've always been more interested in the concrete. A foundation is the least exciting part of any building. It's poured early, it's buried, and nobody posts it. But it decides how tall the building can be, and whether it stands when the weather turns.

At its core, crypto is a new floor for finance. Value can be held without a middleman, moved at any hour, and programmed like any other piece of software. Most of what has been built on that floor so far is noisy, and some of it is nonsense. The question that matters is whether the floor itself keeps getting stronger.

The mood right now

As I write this in late November 2026, the house looks bad.

Market moodAs of 26 Nov 2026
  • 13The Crypto Fear & Greed Index sits at 13, deep in "extreme fear." A month earlier it read 51, neutral.5
  • ~$60KBitcoin trades around $60,000, roughly half its October 2025 peak of about $125,700.3,4
  • $6.3BOn 29 October, Binance lost about $6.3 billion in a record exchange hack, which the FBI attributed to North Korea.6
  • ~$5BUS spot Bitcoin ETFs saw close to $5 billion of outflows in November.4
  • 92%Prediction markets give roughly a 92% chance that the Fed raises rates in December. Inflation is staying sticky, and part of the blame is going to the AI boom.5

That last point is worth sitting with. The two forces this letter is about are already touching: the AI build-out is big enough to move inflation, and inflation is moving crypto.

Look only at the house and the conclusion is easy: stay away. So I looked underneath.

What the numbers don't say

  • It's not a straight line. Bitcoin has fallen more than 50% several times, including right now.
  • Owning is not using. Real stablecoin payments were about $390 billion in 2025, roughly 0.02% of global payment flows.11
  • Growth isn't safety. Collapses, hacks and fraud are part of this history too.

Reading the gap

This is the part of the analysis that matters most to me: sentiment and foundation are telling two different stories.

Sentiment says fear. The foundation says something else:

  • By most counts, more people hold crypto than ever.
  • About $300 billion in digital dollars sits on blockchains, close to its record.
  • Real stablecoin payments grew about 42% in the first eight months of 2026 compared with the same months of 2025.11

Markets price the mood. Foundations follow usage.

In the short run, the mood usually wins. Over years, usage usually does. Not always, and not for every project built on top.

The second floor: work as software

More than a decade after I first read about Bitcoin, I felt the same shift again, this time with AI.

If crypto turns money into software, AI turns work into software. Writing, designing, analyzing, coding and operating are all getting cheaper, fast.

  • Gartner expected 40% of enterprise applications to include task-specific AI agents by the end of 2026, up from under 5% in 2025.12
  • Adobe measured AI-referred visits to US retail sites up 393% year on year in early 2026, and those visitors now buy more often than average.12

One person with good systems can now do what used to take a department. Creation and production are being rebuilt on this second floor. Like the first, most of what you see is noise: demos, hype, fear. The concrete underneath is what matters.

Where the two floors meet

AI agents increasingly act on our behalf: booking, buying, paying for data, paying other agents. To do that, they need money they can hold and move by themselves, at machine speed, under rules we set. Card rails were built for humans with office hours. Programmable money was built for exactly this.

It's already starting. Coinbase's x402 lets an AI agent pay with a stablecoin inside a single web request. Through Coinbase's own service alone, it has processed more than 100 million transactions. Total volume is only about $28 million, which tells you the shape: millions of tiny payments, machines paying machines.13 Google, Stripe with OpenAI, Visa and Mastercard all now run their own agent-payment standards or programs.

The numbers are small. That's what early concrete looks like.

What I'm watching

No price targets here. I don't know where prices go, and neither does anyone else. These are the signals I'll follow through the fear:

  1. 01Real stablecoin payments, not just supply. Usage is the foundation. Supply is just inventory.
  2. 02Agent payment volume. When that $28 million becomes billions, the two floors will have joined.
  3. 03Ownership through the downturn. If the number of owners holds while prices fall, the base is real.
  4. 04Security. The Binance hack began with a fake job interview and an internal AI tool. Custody and security are part of the foundation too.6
  5. 05Rates. If the AI boom keeps inflation sticky, money stays expensive, and that pressure lands on risk assets first.

What I take from it

Foundations are slow and messy, and much of what gets built on them fails. So the approach I keep coming back to is simple:

  • Understand the floor before you get excited about the house.
  • Watch whether the foundation keeps getting stronger, especially when the mood is at its worst.
  • Move carefully, in small steps, with rules written on a calm day.

Concrete gets poured when nobody is watching. Most people only notice the building once it's finished.

Nobody photographs the concrete. But it's the reason anything stays standing.

Quiet money moves like water.
